Sample feasibility review: adjustable-mass bat training collar

Please read: this is a sample written for a hypothetical concept that I invented to show the style and depth of a feasibility opinion. It is not a review of any client's design (real design details come only after scope and NDA). Every number below is an assumption, and the calculations are hand calculations, not tests. It gives no legal, patent or league-rules opinion.

1. Verdict (sample)

Plausible, with three conditions. A four-setting collar that adds 57, 113 or 170 g at about 28 in from the knob changes swing inertia by roughly 10%, 20% and 30% (table 1), which is a meaningful, measurable training effect. The passive centrifugal lockout is plausible because swing accelerations at the collar are large (about 50 to 140 g at 30 to 50 rad/s) compared with the near zero acceleration when the dial is turned by hand. The conditions: (1) the lockout threshold must sit in a usable window; (2) the collar must survive ball impact and still retain its mass; (3) the retention and failure mode must be safe, because a loose 170 g collar at swing speed carries about 41 J.

Lockout threshold windowMust stay free at 0 g (dial turned by hand), engage before a real swing, and not jam on slow dry swings (about 13 to 22 g at 15 to 20 rad/s).HighSpin-rig test across 10 to 50 rad/s; spring-rate and pawl-mass tuning; add a positive detent so the dial cannot move.

5. Is a bench or pendulum test worth doing? (sample opinion)

Yes, in two steps. (a) A compound pendulum on the bat with the collar fitted at each setting costs little and gives the real swing inertia and balance, which checks table 1. (b) A spin rig (a slow motor-driven arm with the collar mounted at the correct radius) up to about 50 rad/s checks the lockout threshold and retention. An impact test is a third, later step. I would not spend on a full prototype before step (a) and a spin rig show the lockout window is real.
